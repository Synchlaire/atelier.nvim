## Design Context

### Users
Atelier serves keyboard-first Neovim users who maintain several colorschemes and want to browse, preview, install, update, and persist them without leaving the editor. They value speed, predictable state, and commands that remain understandable under repetition.

### Brand Personality
Tactile, precise, and quietly expressive. Atelier should feel like a well-used workshop instrument: responsive under the fingers, honest about what it is doing, and warm without becoming whimsical.

### Aesthetic Direction
Preserve the restrained monochrome, technical interface and semantic terminal colors. Add personality through material language, compact state markers, responsive density, and satisfying confirmations. Support both dark and light colorschemes. Avoid glassy effects, decorative animation, novelty copy, excessive icons, and anything that competes with theme evaluation.

### Design Principles
1. Make preview, applied, persisted, busy, and failed states visibly distinct.
2. Treat every theme change as a reversible transaction until explicitly applied.
3. Reveal advanced maintenance actions progressively and confirm destructive work.
4. Size the interface to its contents while remaining usable in small terminals and large collections.
5. Add delight through crisp feedback and workshop-like language, never delay or obscure the task.
